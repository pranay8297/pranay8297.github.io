01 / Story

Boston made the work feel physical.

I went to Northeastern to study applied machine intelligence, but Boston gave me a more specific question: what happens when learning systems touch the real world?

I lived in a city where biotech is not an abstraction. At Arrakis Therapeutics, my co-op sat inside the therapeutic discovery loop: molecular strings, fingerprints, graph neural networks, solubility prediction, distributed training, and the pressure to make research iteration faster.

That experience changed how I judge AI work. I care about the model, but I care just as much about the surrounding system: how data enters, how errors are contained, how privacy is protected, how quickly a researcher or operator can try the next idea, and whether the system still behaves when it leaves the notebook.

02 / Research

The thread I keep pulling is generation under constraints.

Robotics

Behavior synthesis with diffusion models

I built a diffusion-based behavior synthesis model for robotic control because path planning is not only about reaching a point; it is about producing trajectories that remain useful under uncertainty. I optimized the model until it was small enough and fast enough to matter in real-time planning.

50% smaller model 62% faster inference Concurrent action generation
Most real deployments do not have infinite compute. That constraint makes the research more interesting.
Protein design

ESM-Decoder for low-resource adaptation

I adapted Meta's ESM model into an auto-regressive decoder for protein sequence modeling. The motivation was practical: make strong biological sequence models easier to adapt without paying the full compute bill every time a new experiment appears.

70% model-size reduction 97% fewer FLOPs with LoRA 650M and 3B model fine-tuning
Generative modeling

Denoising diffusion across modalities

I implemented DDPM, Karras-style diffusion, flow-based generative models, and spectrogram diffusion for audio. I kept returning to the same engineering question: can a sampler keep quality while taking fewer steps?

Sampling reduced to 10 steps 35 to 15 function evaluations FID 4.1 on Fashion-MNIST
Molecular ML

Graph learning at Arrakis Therapeutics

In Boston's biotech ecosystem, I trained models on molecular data, advanced graph-based molecular property prediction, and extended HiGNN with chemical bond data and contrastive pre-training. The goal was not novelty for its own sake; it was better prediction and faster iteration for therapeutic discovery.

7% better solubility prediction 3M-compound distributed training 60% faster deployment iteration
03 / Systems

I like research most when it survives contact with production.

Now

At Emtribe, I architect AI-native products end to end.

I am the tech lead and sole architect behind Vaayu and emFI, two flagship products built from scratch. My role is not limited to implementation: I define the product shape, draw the user flows, make architecture decisions, build core systems, and hold the line between what the product promises and what the engineering can safely deliver.

Vaayu is significant because sustainability work is usually trapped in spreadsheets, manual collection, and brittle reporting. I built the platform around ingestion, computation, and reporting pipelines so emissions and ESG data can become a working system instead of a quarterly scramble. The hard part is not just storing the data; it is making calculations repeatable, auditable, and usable by teams that are not engineers.

emFI is the more radical product. Sustainable finance usually asks borrowers and lenders to move through rigid forms, document checklists, and opaque workflows. I am building it as a structureless, AI-native platform where a copilot becomes the primary interface: users prompt to onboard a project, submit an application, complete diligence, surface missing information, or move a financing workflow forward without first learning how the software is organized.

Privacy

I design AI systems around data boundaries.

For emFI, I built privacy walls, PII masking, and in-house LLM deployment so sensitive data could stay resident. The model is only one part of the product; the trust boundary is part of the architecture.

Edge

I compress models until they can work where the cloud cannot.

I built an on-device OCR engine for smart meters at remote offline sites. The pipeline runs three models in sequence under 80MB main memory on legacy hardware, reaching 99% accuracy without depending on connectivity.

Civic

I used LLMs to make public process easier to read.

At MAPLE in Boston, I built RAG pipelines for legislative bill summaries, automated tagging, and testimony summarization. The system processed more than 1000 testimonies a month and helped turn dense civic documents into something easier to track and act on.
